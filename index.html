About

Biomovies is a 3D biological structure video editor. This cross-platform, open-source software allows the creation of movies starting from a 3D biological structure file. Additionally, the application accepts trajectories coupled with its corresponding topology.

Thanks to its simplicity and the intuitive interface, users can build videos easily and quickly.

The application allows the creation of low resolution and HD videos as well as animated images. The projects can be saved for the sake of sharing or editing them later. Templates can be exported too, allowing to apply the same edition to different biological structures.

The project provides a broad documentation as well as demonstration videos and tutorials.

p53 as AlphaFold predicts it

The AlphaFold model of human p53 (AF-P04637-F1), coloured by confidence: blue where the prediction is confident, yellow and orange where it is not. The long disordered tails float around a DNA-binding domain that folds with high confidence.

The MS2 phage capsid

The capsid of bacteriophage MS2 (PDB 2MS2): 180 copies of one 129-residue coat protein tile the shell in three shapes, which differ in one loop (residues 66–82). Then the inner face, the one that holds the RNA genome.

Colibactin on DNA

A molecular dynamics simulation of colibactin, the genotoxin made by some E. coli strains, bound to a 16-base-pair DNA duplex, with a close-up of the bases it touches. Simulation: MDposit MD-A00195 (Orozco lab, IRB Barcelona).

Haemoglobin and its hemes

Haemoglobin (PDB 4HHB): four chains, four hemes. Then a close-up of one heme in an α chain: His87 binds the iron, His58 faces the oxygen site.

An enzyme and its ligand in motion

A molecular dynamics simulation of diphosphomevalonate decarboxylase from Staphylococcus epidermidis (PDB 4DPY) with its ligand: a close-up of the ligand, the residues within 5 Å and their interactions. Simulation: MDposit MD-A003OP (MDBind dataset).

Three k-turns make a triangle

Three k-turn RNA units assemble into a triangle (PDB 5G4U). In one k-turn, the GAA bulge kinks the helix, and the L7Ae protein grips the bulge and locks the kink.

The NLRP3 cage in motion

A coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulation of the NLRP3 inflammasome cage (PDB 7PZC), ten copies of NLRP3, then one copy alone with the other nine faded. Simulation: MDposit mmb-A02JN, SIRAH force field (M4D group, University of Torino; University of Pavia).

The HCN2 channel in a membrane

The rat HCN2 channel: four subunits around one pore, their cyclic nucleotide-binding domains, the pore seen from the cytoplasm, and 800 ns of molecular dynamics in a POPC membrane. Simulation: MDposit mmb-A05DL (M4D group, University of Torino).

A nucleosome in motion

A molecular dynamics simulation of a nucleosome, DNA wrapped on the histone core, with the two H3 histones highlighted and the rest faded. Simulation: MDposit MD-A001KC, MMTV nucleosome A (Bishop lab, Louisiana Tech University).

The Shine-Dalgarno helix

The 70S ribosome with its mRNA and tRNAs (PDB 4V4J): the large and small subunits, then the Shine-Dalgarno helix, where 8 base pairs join the mRNA (AAGGAGGU) to the 3′ end of the 16S rRNA (ACCUCCUU).

SARS-CoV-2 spike: the RBD opens and closes

A molecular dynamics simulation of the SARS-CoV-2 spike: its receptor-binding domain (RBD) opens, then closes, shown on one chain with the other two as a surface.

TBP bends the TATA box

The TATA-binding protein bends the DNA of the TATA box (PDB 1CDW). Its arginines and lysines grip the DNA backbone, and arginines form salt bridges with the phosphates.

Your first molecular movie

Your first molecular movie, from a PDB code to a video file: HIV-1 protease with its inhibitor (1HSG), the camera keyframes of a clip, a cloned clip as a close-up, a transition between them, a fade out and the export (up to 4K, in 3D, or as an animated GIF). The captions are in the video.

Point at what matters

A close-up that tells its story: the residues around a ligand and its interactions, a label with one click, a distance between two atoms and a text over the movie. Then only the close-up exported as a GIF for your slides. The captions are in the video.

A simulation movie from a template

A whole movie of a molecular dynamics simulation in one go: a ready-made template, any simulation of the MDposit database by its accession, a title, the trajectory while the camera turns and a fade. Then your own title, and which frames of the trajectory play, and how fast. The captions are in the video.

Ask the assistant

Describe the movie you want and the assistant builds it in the editor as you watch: your own provider and key, set up once (kept encrypted on your computer), a request in your words, Preview, a follow-up and Undo all of it. Also: other apps through MCP (Claude Code, Claude Desktop, opencode, VS Code). The captions are in the video.

The representation options

What each clip shows, and how: selections from a list or your own (residues, chains, atoms), opacity, the styles and the colours, interactions, a representation copied to another clip, and residues picked in the sequences. The captions are in the video.

The timeline at a glance

Find your way around the timeline: its rows, the cursor, playing and zooming; selecting keyframes and whole items, dragging, splitting and removing them; undo one step at a time; and aligning the camera of two keyframes. The captions are in the video.
